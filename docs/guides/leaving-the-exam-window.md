# Leaving the exam window

> 한국어로 읽기: [시험 창을 벗어나면](leaving-the-exam-window-ko.md)

During an exam, keep the exam window in front: don't switch to another window, tab or app. If you do leave it, for a second
or longer, here is exactly what happens. In short: **the exam pauses, your words are kept, and a person, never the computer,
decides whether anything needs following up.**

## While you are away

- **The exam pauses.** The examiner stops speaking, and the answer you were giving waits: the words you had already said are
  kept, and nothing is sent automatically while you are away.
- The page says: *The exam is paused while this window is in the background. Come back to this window to carry on.*
- The exam clock is kept on the server and isn't stopped, so come back as soon as you can.

## When you come back

- A neutral line says the exam paused while the window was in the background.
- The examiner repeats the end of the question (the whole question if you left while it was being asked) and you carry on
  where you were.
- There is no strike counter, no warning sound and no penalty on the screen.

## What is noted

Each absence of a second or more is noted: when it happened, how long it lasted and during which question. Shorter blips
(for example a click on the browser's own toolbar) are ignored.

If the window is left **three or more times, or once for a minute or more**, the attempt is marked for your instructor to look
at. You are told straight away, with a box where you can explain what happened (optional; only your instructor sees it). Things
like a notification, a system dialog or a screen reader can take the focus away by themselves, so your explanation helps.

## What never happens

- **Your exam is never cancelled automatically**, and nothing is deleted. Your answers and your transcript are always kept.
- The notes are never part of your grade and never a finding of cheating on their own. A person reads them alongside your
  answers and decides.
- Your screen is never recorded.

This follows data-protection law and regulators' guidance in Europe: a computer alone must not interrupt an exam or decide
an outcome; a person has to.

## If you need to leave the window

If assistive technology takes the focus away from the exam window, or you have a documented need to step away, ask your
instructor for the **leaving-the-window accommodation** before the exam. They can raise the number or length of absences
before an attempt is marked for review, or turn the mark off for you. The exam still pauses while you are away and absences
are still noted. See [Accommodations](accommodations.md).

## Tips

- Close other apps and browser tabs before you begin, and turn on "do not disturb" so notifications don't pull the focus away.
- Keep the exam in one browser window, on the screen you look at.
