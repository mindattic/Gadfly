# Practice interview: meet your examiner

**Meet your examiner** is a short practice with the talking examiner who gives your exams. She explains each part of a
real exam and has you try it, out loud and on camera, exactly as in the exam room. It takes about five minutes.

**Nothing from the practice is kept.** It isn't graded, reported or counted as an attempt, it doesn't touch any of your
exams, and no instructor sees it. You can take it as often as you like, at any time, even before you have an exam.

## Starting it

Open **My exams** and press **Start the practice** on the **Meet your examiner (practice)** card. Read the short
introduction and press **Start the practice** again. Your browser asks to use your microphone and camera: allow both.
You need your sound on too; a headset works best.

At the top you see the **practice clock**, which step you are on, and three links: **Skip this step** (if you get stuck),
**Start again** and **Leave the practice**.

## The steps

The examiner speaks each step, with captions. The same advice is shown under her, so you can read it at your own pace.

1. **Speaking an answer.** She asks an easy warm-up question. Answer out loud; your words appear under **What I heard**.
   Stop talking and a short countdown appears; when it runs out, your answer is sent. Speaking again cancels it, so you
   can pause to think. **Done answering** sends it at once and **Repeat the question** has her say it again.
2. **Your answer is taken as heard.** She doesn't read your answer back, and you can't confirm, correct or change it
   afterwards: once it is sent, the exam moves on, like a conversation with a person. So say what you mean before the
   countdown ends. Until then you can keep talking, or press **Clear and answer again** to start over: she has you try
   that. If a word is misheard you don't need to do anything; the person who grades your exam sees which words the
   recognizer was unsure of.
3. **Saying "I don't know".** After each answer she takes a moment. You may hear a short neutral line such as "Let me
   think" and a quiet sound of pages turning: that is her thinking, and it says nothing about how your answer went. Then
   she asks something you probably can't answer, so you can practise saying **"I don't know"**. It is an honest, real
   answer and never fails you on its own; in an exam she tries another angle or moves on.
4. **Eye contact and the screen fade.** While you answer, look at her, as you would with a person. She has you look away
   for a few seconds mid-answer so you can see the screen slowly fade with **Please look at the examiner**, and clear as
   soon as you look back. Keep looking away and she asks you out loud to look at her. Short glances while you think are
   fine. See [Eye contact and the screen fade](eye-contact-and-the-screen-fade.md).
5. **If the microphone or camera stops.** If either stops working, or the connection drops, the exam shows **The exam
   is paused** with what went wrong and how to fix it; you fix it and press **Try again**, and she asks the question
   again. The clock stops while you are paused, for up to 10 minutes in all. You can try it: unplug your microphone or
   turn off your camera, put it back and press **Try again**, and watch the practice clock stop and the paused time
   come back. When you're done, say "ready".
6. **You're ready.** A last word from her, then **Go to My exams** (or **Practice again**).

## What is the same as an exam, and what isn't

The same: the examiner, her voice and captions, the microphone and its countdown, your camera picture, the transcript,
the response delay, the eye-contact check and fade, and the pause when a device stops.

Different:

- Her questions are fixed practice questions, not about your course, and your answers are not judged. Only one thing is
  checked, on the spot: whether you said "I don't know", so she can reply to it.
- Nothing is stored: no answers, no eye-contact notes, no attempt. (In an exam a short note of a look-away is kept for
  a person to read.) As in an exam, your camera picture stays on your device and no video or sound is recorded.
- The eye-contact check always runs here, even if an exam of yours has it turned off or you have an eye-contact
  accommodation; in your exam your accommodation applies.
- The practice clock is only there so you can see it stop during a pause. Nothing happens when it runs out.

## If it doesn't start

The practice needs what an exam needs: a current browser, a working microphone and camera, and the talking examiner
turned on for your school. If the examiner can't start, the page says why and offers **Try again**. See
[microphone problems](troubleshooting-microphone.md), [camera problems](troubleshooting-camera.md) and
[browser problems](troubleshooting-browser.md).

On a demonstration server running in practice mode, speech recognition is simulated, so your words appear as the
placeholder "speech". Everything else works the same.
