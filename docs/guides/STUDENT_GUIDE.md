# Student guide

Welcome. This guide explains how a Gadfly exam works, how to get ready for one, what happens in the exam room,
and what happens to your answers afterwards. It is also in the app: press **Help** in the top bar, or open the
help pages from the sign-in screen without signing in.

> **Tip.** Short on time? Read the [quick start](quick-start-student.md) and the
> [checklist for the day](before-your-exam.md). Come back here when you want the detail.

## What a Gadfly exam is

A Gadfly exam is a **spoken conversation**, not a written test. A talking examiner on your screen asks you one
question at a time, out loud, about the topics your instructor chose. You answer out loud, in your own words, with
your camera on. The examiner builds each next question from **what you just said**. It might:

- ask you to explain an idea in your own words;
- ask what you meant by a word you used;
- ask *why*, or what would happen if something changed;
- offer a tricky case that doesn't fit your explanation;
- give you a new situation where you have to use the idea, not just define it;
- ask how two ideas connect;
- point out, politely, when two of your answers seem to disagree.

That is the method Socrates used: keep asking until it is clear what you understand and what you don't. Each
answer is checked against your course material by a panel of several AI models, but **people decide your grade**:
the AI's view is only preliminary. [How the examiner asks questions](how-the-examiner-asks-questions.md) goes
into this in more depth.

## How to prepare

**Understand, don't memorize.** Because each question grows out of your last answer, you can't predict the
questions, and a memorized definition only gets you through the first one. What helps:

- For each topic, practise explaining it **out loud**, as if to a friend. Speaking is different from writing, and
  the exam is spoken.
- Ask yourself *why* each fact is true, and what would change if it weren't.
- Think of an example, and a case that is *not* an example.
- Look for links between topics.

## Signing in

You get into Gadfly in one of two ways:

- **An invitation email.** It says you have been invited to join your organization and has a link that works
  once. The link opens **Join your organization**, where you choose a password (at least 12 characters; your user
  name is your email address unless you pick another). Then sign in.
- **A user name and temporary password** from your instructor or administrator. At your first sign-in you are
  asked to **Choose a new password**.

Forgot your password? Press **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page and enter your user name or email. If an
account matches, you get an email with a link to choose a new one.

Students don't need two-step verification (an authenticator app); staff do.

## Finding your exam: My exams

Sign in and open **My exams**. You are not emailed when an exam is assigned to you: it simply appears here. Each
exam shows:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Not open yet** | The exam has an opening time that hasn't arrived. The opening time is shown underneath. |
| **Ready to start** | You can start it now. |
| **In progress** | You started an attempt and haven't finished it. Press **Resume**. |
| **Submitted** | Your answers are in. Your result appears here once it has been graded, approved and published. |
| **Closed** | The exam's window has closed. |
| **No longer available** / **Not available yet** | Your instructor has archived the exam, or it isn't ready. |

You also see the window when the exam is open (times are shown in UTC), **Attempts used X of Y**, and any
instructions from your instructor. If your instructor closes an exam to new attempts while you are part-way
through one, the exam says so and you can still finish the attempt you started (but not after its window closes).
Each exam also says how you answer it: **Spoken exam, on camera**, or, if your instructor granted typed answers,
**Spoken questions, typed answers (your accommodation)**.

### Practise first: meet your examiner

At the top of **My exams** is **Meet your examiner (practice)**, a five-minute run-through with the talking examiner,
open at any time, even before you have an exam. She has you try answering out loud, saying "I don't know", keeping eye
contact (and seeing the screen fade), and what happens if your microphone or camera stops. Nothing from it is kept,
graded or counted as an attempt. See [Practice interview](practice-interview.md).

## The get-ready page

Press **Start**. The get-ready page has three steps. Nothing is recorded on this page, and no attempt is used
until you press **Begin exam**: if you leave now, you can come back later.

### 1. System check

Every exam is spoken and on camera, so the page checks your setup. Allow your **microphone and camera** when your
browser asks. You see your own camera picture and a microphone level meter; **say a few words** so the meter moves.

| Check | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Browser** | A browser that can keep a live connection: a current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. |
| **Cookies** | Cookies allowed for this site. |
| **Connection** | A connection fast enough for a conversation. "Slow" is a warning you can carry on with; "too slow" or "offline" must be fixed. |
| **Screen** | A window at least 360 pixels wide. A very narrow window is a warning, not a stop. |
| **Spoken examiner** | The talking examiner switched on for this site. If it says "not turned on for this site", only your instructor or administrator can fix it. |
| **Microphone** | A working microphone that has **heard you**. |
| **Camera** | A working camera that shows a picture. |

Each check shows ✓ (fine), **!** (a warning; you can still begin), ✗ (must be fixed) or … (still waiting). Under
anything marked ✗ the page says how to fix it. Fix it, then press **Check again**. **Begin exam** stays off until
every check passes. If something won't pass, see [microphone problems](troubleshooting-microphone.md),
[camera problems](troubleshooting-camera.md) or [browser problems](troubleshooting-browser.md).

### 2. How Socratic questioning works

A short explanation of the method (open **Why it works this way** for the research behind it) and one practice
question: *"in your own words, what is a habit?"* Say your answer out loud and press **I've said my answer**, or
try **I don't know** to see what happens. The practice isn't recorded or graded, and you don't have to do it.

### 3. Before you begin: the notice

A plain-language notice for this exam: what is recorded, what isn't, how long it is kept, who decides your
result, and the rules for pauses and time. Read it, tick **I have read this notice and agree to take the exam on
these terms**, and press **Begin exam** (it says **Continue to the exam** if you already started an attempt).

A fingerprint of the exact wording you agreed to is saved with your exam, so there is a record of what you were
told. If you don't want to agree, simply leave the page: nothing is started and no attempt is used.

## In the exam room

**Begin exam** takes you straight to the talking examiner. Your browser may ask once more for the microphone and
camera: allow both. If you reload the page later, you may see **One click to go**: press **Continue to the
examiner** (browsers need one click before a page can speak).

What you see:

- **The examiner**, who speaks each question aloud with captions, and **your own camera** beside it, labelled
  "only you see this". The picture stays on your screen; it is not recorded or sent.
- The **question as text**, with the topic it is about.
- The **countdown** (it turns red in the last two minutes) and **Topics finished X of Y**.
- A **Transcript** that fills in as you go: each question, and each answer as it was heard.
- **Response delay**: how long you took to start speaking. It is shown for your information.

If your device has no 3D graphics, or asks for reduced motion, you see the examiner's picture instead of the moving
head. The questions are still spoken and shown as text.

### Answering

**Answer out loud, in your own words.** Short, honest answers are better than long recited ones. Your words appear
under **What I heard** as you speak.

- **Ending an answer.** Stop talking and a short countdown appears; when it runs out, your answer is sent.
  Speaking again cancels it, so you can pause to think mid-answer. The pause it waits for starts at about four
  seconds and adjusts to how you speak (more if you have extra time). You can also press **Done answering**.
- **Clear and answer again** throws away what you've said so far for this answer, so you can start over.
- **Repeat the question** has the examiner say it again.
- **Switch to hold-to-talk** if your room is noisy: then only what you say while holding **Hold to talk** counts.
  **Switch to hands-free** goes back.
- **I don't know** is a real answer. Socrates called that moment *aporia*: honest puzzlement, the start of real
  learning. The examiner notes it and tries another angle or moves on.

### Your answer is taken as heard

Once your answer is sent, it is taken as the examiner heard it and the exam moves on, like a conversation with a
person. The examiner doesn't read your answer back, and there is no step to confirm, correct, repeat or change it
afterwards: a real-time answer is what the exam is about. So say what you mean before the countdown sends it; until
then you can keep talking, or use **Clear and answer again** to start over.

If a word was misheard, you don't need to do anything: a person reviewing your exam sees which words the speech
recognizer was unsure of, and graders are told to ignore recognition errors that don't change your meaning. If the
next question shows the examiner misunderstood you, say so in your next answer and put it again in your own words.

After each answer the examiner takes a few seconds to think ("Thinking about your answer…"). Keep the page open.
You won't see any scores during the exam.

### Eye contact

While you answer, keep your eyes on the examiner, as you would with a person. If you look away for a while, the
screen slowly fades and the examiner may ask you to look back; look at the examiner and it clears at once. Short
glances while you think are fine, and you get extra time at the start of an answer. A short note of when it
happened is kept for a person to read alongside your answer; it is never a verdict and never part of your grade.
[Eye contact and the screen fade](eye-contact-and-the-screen-fade.md) explains exactly how it works. If you can't
keep eye contact (for example because of a disability), ask your instructor for the eye-contact
[accommodation](accommodations.md) before the exam.

### If the microphone, camera or connection stops

Your microphone and camera must stay on. If either stops working (unplugged, blocked, taken by another app) or the
connection to the examiner drops, the exam shows **The exam is paused** with what went wrong and how to fix it.
Fix it and press **Try again**; the examiner repeats the question.

The clock stops while the exam is paused, for up to **10 minutes in all** (or half the exam time, if that is
shorter). After that it keeps running.

If something goes wrong on our side you see "Your progress is saved; press Try again to continue". If an exam
can't continue at all, ask your instructor to reset it.

## Time

- Your time starts when the examiner begins asking the first question, not when you press **Begin exam**.
- The time limit is set by your instructor and kept on the server, so the clock **keeps running** if you close the
  page. If you reload or lose your connection, sign back in and you return to the same question.
- When time runs out the exam ends; an answer you are still speaking is not handed in. An answer that arrives more
  than about 20 seconds after time is up isn't judged.
- The exam can also end early when every topic is covered.
- **Finish and leave the exam** ends it now (you are asked **Finish the exam now?** first). What you've answered
  so far is submitted and that attempt is used up; you can't come back to it.

When the exam ends you see **Your answers were submitted** and why it ended. Press **Back to my exams**.

## What is recorded, and for how long

| Recorded | Not recorded |
|---|---|
| Every question, and a transcript of your spoken answers, as they were heard | Video: no camera picture ever leaves your device |
| When each question was asked and when you started and finished answering | Audio: your voice is sent to a speech-to-text service to make the transcript, and not kept |
| How you spoke: pace, pauses, hesitations, how sure the speech recognizer was, and whether more than one voice was heard | Your screen |
| Short notes about eye contact while you answered (when you looked away, how long, and whether the screen faded) | |
| The AI panel's preliminary judgment of each answer | |
| Integrity notes for a person to read, such as an answer that reads very differently from your others | |
| Browser notes, unless your instructor turned them off: switching to another tab or window (and for how long), copying the question, and on some exams leaving full screen or having a second screen connected | |
| Your consent: the time, and a fingerprint of the notice you accepted | |

Integrity notes are for a person to read in context. **They never fail you automatically, are never part of your
grade, and nothing is blocked.** The notice before each exam lists exactly what that exam notes.

**Voice measurements.** Unless your organization or your instructor turned them off, the exam room also measures
a few things about your voice (pitch, steadiness, loudness) and compares each answer with your own first answers.
You see a short line about it in your own transcript. It describes your voice, never your feelings or honesty, may
make the examiner ask a further question, and never changes your grade. In the EU and EEA it is **off** by default.

Your answers are kept for your organization's retention period, shown in the notice (90 days unless your
organization set another), and then removed. The outcome (the judgments, your result and the examiner's comments)
is kept. [Privacy in plain words](privacy-in-plain-words.md) has the full picture.

## Your results

After the exam, people grade your answers: an examiner scores them independently of the AI, large differences
between the two are reconciled by a reviewer, and an authorized person approves the grade before it is published.
[How grading and results work](grading-and-results.md) explains each step.

When your result is published, **My exams** shows **Your result**: your grade as a percentage (with its label, if
your organization uses one), a note if it was amended or an appeal is under review, and **See the assessment
report**. The report shows, for each learning objective, the scores, the examiner's comment, what was missing, and
the parts of your answers used as evidence. **Download the report (Markdown)** or **Download the report (PDF)** saves
a copy.

**Download your completion certificate (PDF)** saves a one-page certificate with your name, the exam, your result
and its own verification code, checked the same way as your transcript's code below.

Until then, a submitted exam shows: "Your answers were submitted. Your result appears here once examiners have
graded it and it has been approved and published."

The result page also shows **Integrity notes on this attempt**: anything the checks noted during your exam (for
example a long look away from the examiner, a second face in view, or the exam tab hidden), when it happened, during
which question, and the same plain explanation your instructor reads. Brief, ordinary moments are only counted. These
notes are context for a person, never a finding of cheating, and none of them is part of your grade. They appear only
once your result is published.

**Disagree with your result, or with an integrity note?** You can appeal from the result page, and respond to the
integrity notes in the same appeal. See [Appeals](appeals.md).

## Your transcript

Once your result is published, the result page also offers **Your transcript**: the official record of your exam.
It lists every question with its time and how far into the exam it came, each of your answers exactly as it was
recorded, any question about what you meant and your reply, any correction you made (the original and the corrected
text side by side; spoken answers taken since November 2026 have none, since they are kept as heard), how long you
waited before answering and your
pauses, and the words the speech recognizer found hard to hear, marked [like this?]. Download it as a PDF, a Word
document, a page to print, plain text, captions (VTT or SRT) or JSON.

Your copy leaves out the notes meant only for reviewers (camera, sound and browser observations, voice measurements
and the AI's exam-time scores). Every copy carries a **verification code** such as `7K3M-9QXA-B2CD-EFGH`: anyone
you give it to (a school, an employer) can type the code at `/transcripts/verify`, or upload the JSON file there, to
check it is genuine and unchanged. That page never shows what your transcript says, and it checks a certificate's
code the same way. A file you download is yours to keep; your organization's retention rules do not reach copies you
have saved.

## Your data

You can download a copy of everything Gadfly holds about you in your organization (your assignments, attempts,
questions and answers, judgments, integrity notes, reports and grades): press **Download my data** at the top of
**My exams**. It is a single JSON file. If you belong to several organizations, each one's copy is separate;
switch organization first (the drop-down in the top bar).

To have your exam records deleted, ask your organization's administrator. Deletion removes your exam records in
that organization and can't be undone.

## Research (optional)

If your organization is running a study of these exams, the get-ready page asks, below the exam notice, whether your
de-identified exam records may be included. It is entirely optional: you can skip it or say no, and your exam is the
same, graded the same way, and the people grading you are not told what you chose. The study never gets your name,
email, what you said or typed, audio or video; you appear under a study code. You can change your mind at any time on
**My research choice** (linked from **My exams**): join, decline, or withdraw if you had agreed. Withdrawing stops your
records going into any later copy for the study.

## Email

Gadfly can email you when an exam is assigned, as a reminder before it's due, when a result is released, and about
your own appeals. Press **Email preferences** next to **Download my data** on **My exams** to turn off the
before-it's-due reminder — the result-released email and the others still come through, since they matter. Your
organization can also turn off Gadfly's emails entirely; if they have, your own choice here won't change anything.

## Getting help

- Something not working? Start with the [FAQ](faq.md) and the troubleshooting pages under **Fixing problems** in
  Help.
- Questions about your exam, your attempts, your window or your result: ask your instructor.
- Accounts, passwords that won't reset, or deleting your data: ask your organization's administrator.
