# Student guide

Welcome. This page explains how a Gadfly exam works, how to get ready, and what happens to your answers.

## What a Socratic exam is

It's a conversation, not a multiple-choice test. An AI examiner asks you one question at a time about the
topics your instructor chose, and builds the next question from **what you just said**. It might:

- ask you to explain an idea in your own words;
- ask what you meant by a word you used;
- ask *why*, or what would happen if something changed;
- offer a tricky case that doesn't fit your explanation;
- give you a new situation where you have to use the idea, not just define it;
- ask how two ideas connect;
- point out, politely, when two of your answers seem to disagree.

That's the method Socrates used: keep asking until it's clear what you understand and what you don't.
Every answer is checked against your course material by several AI models, not just one, and your
instructor reads your answers and decides your result.

## How to prepare

**Understand, don't memorize.** Because each question grows out of your last answer, you can't predict
the questions, and a memorized definition only gets you through the first one. What helps:

- For each topic, practise explaining it out loud or on paper in your own words, as if to a friend.
- Ask yourself *why* each fact is true, and what would change if it weren't.
- Think of an example, and a case that is *not* an example.
- Look for links between topics.

## Before you start: the system check and the notice

Sign in, open **My exams** and press **Start**. The get-ready page has three steps.

1. **System check.** Your browser, cookies, internet connection and screen are checked. If something is
   marked ✗, the page tells you how to fix it; press **Check again** afterwards. A slow connection gets a
   warning but still works for typed answers. Microphone and camera checks show "coming with voice mode":
   you don't need either today.
2. **How it works.** A short explanation and one practice question. The practice isn't recorded.
3. **Notice and consent.** A plain-language notice: what is recorded, what isn't, how long it's kept and
   who decides your result. Read it, tick the box, and press **Begin exam**. A fingerprint of the exact
   wording you agreed to is saved with your exam, so there's a record of what you were told.

## During the exam

- You see one question at a time, the topic it's about, a countdown and how many topics are finished.
- **Answer in your own words.** Short, honest answers are better than long copied ones. Type your answer
  and press **Submit answer** (or Ctrl+Enter). You can't change an answer after submitting it.
- **"I don't know" is a real answer.** If you genuinely don't know, press **I don't know**. Socrates
  called that moment *aporia*: honest puzzlement, the start of real learning. It isn't a failure; the
  examiner notes it and tries another angle or moves on.
- After each answer the examiner takes a few seconds to think. Keep the page open.
- You won't see scores during the exam.

## Time

- The time limit is set by your instructor and kept on the server, so the clock **keeps running** if you
  close the page. If you reload or lose your connection, sign back in and you return to the same question.
- When time runs out, whatever you've typed in the box is handed in and the exam ends. An answer that
  arrives more than about 20 seconds after time is up isn't judged.
- The exam can also end early when every topic is covered.
- **Finish and leave the exam** ends it now. What you've answered so far is submitted and that attempt is
  used up; you can't come back to it.

Your instructor sets how many attempts you have and the window when the exam is open; both show on
**My exams**.

## What is recorded, and for how long

| Recorded | Not recorded |
|---|---|
| Every question and your typed answers | Video: none is stored |
| When each question was asked and when you started and finished answering | Your screen |
| The AI panel's judgment of each answer | Any guess about your emotions or honesty: the platform never tries |
| Integrity notes for your instructor, such as an answer that reads very differently from your others | |
| Your consent (the time and a fingerprint of the notice you accepted) | |

Integrity notes are for a person to read in context. They never fail you automatically. Looking away from
the screen while you think is normal and isn't counted against you.

Your answers are kept for your organization's retention period, shown in the notice (90 days unless your
organization set another), and then removed. The outcome (the judgments, your result and your
instructor's comment) is kept.

> Voice answers and camera checks are planned. When they arrive, the notice will say what they record;
> camera checks will run on your own device and only short notes (for example "face not visible for 10
> seconds") will be sent.

## Your results

After the exam, your instructor reads your answers and decides your grade. The AI only recommends. When
your instructor releases your result, **My exams** shows:

- your grade and your instructor's comment;
- the examiner's recommendation in plain words ("Understanding shown", "Partly shown" or "Not yet shown").

Until then the exam shows "Your answers were submitted. Your instructor will release results."

## Your data

You can ask for a copy of everything Gadfly holds about you in your organization (your assignments,
attempts, questions and answers, judgments, integrity notes, reports and grades), or ask for it to be
deleted. Ask your organization's administrator; the copy is a single file, and deletion removes your exam
records in that organization. A download button for students isn't built yet.
