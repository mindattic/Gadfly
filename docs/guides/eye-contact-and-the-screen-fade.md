# Eye contact and the screen fade

In a spoken exam with a person, you'd look at them while you answer. Gadfly asks the same: keep your eyes on the
examiner while you speak. If you look away for a while, the screen gently fades until you look back. This page
explains exactly when that happens, what is kept, and what it does **not** mean.

## When the check runs

The eye-contact check runs on exams with integrity notes switched on (most exams; your notice says so). It works
only while **you are answering**: while the examiner is speaking or thinking, you can look wherever you like.

At the start, a short message asks you to **look at the examiner's face for a moment**, so the camera learns where
the examiner is on your screen. If you move your screen or your chair later, press **Set up eye contact again**.

A small indicator tells you what the camera sees: "Looking at the examiner", "Looking away from the examiner",
"Your face isn't in view of the camera", and so on.

## What makes the screen fade

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| A short glance away while you think | Nothing. Glancing away to recall something is normal. |
| Looking away for **about 2 seconds** in the middle of an answer | The screen starts to fade, and "Please look at the examiner." appears. |
| Looking away at the **start** of an answer | You get more time first (about 6 seconds), because people often look away to gather their thoughts. |
| Looking away longer (about 5 seconds, or 9 at the start) | The examiner may say "Please look at me while you answer." |
| Eyes moving as if reading | The screen may fade, and the examiner may ask you to look at it rather than reading. |
| No face in view, or a second face in view | The examiner asks you to come back into view, or to make sure you are answering on your own. |

The fade builds up over a few seconds and clears **as soon as you look back**. It never blocks the buttons, and it
never stops the exam. The examiner's remark is not recorded as part of your answer (the microphone pauses while it
speaks), and it repeats at most every 15 seconds.

## What is kept

- **No picture or video, ever.** The camera is analysed on your own device. No image leaves it.
- A **short note** of each episode: what kind it was (looked away, reading-like movement, face not in view, a
  second face, the screen faded), when it started and how long it lasted. These are marked as low-confidence.

The notes are for a person reading your exam afterwards, next to the answer you were giving at the time.

## What it does not mean

- It is **never a verdict.** Looking away can mean thinking, nerves, a noise in the room, or a habit. Reviewers are
  told exactly that.
- It is **never part of your grade.** No score is calculated from it.
- It is **hidden from the examiner who grades you blind**, until their grade is in.
- It doesn't try to read your emotions or decide whether you are honest.

## If eye contact is hard for you

Some people can't comfortably hold eye contact with a screen, for example because of autism, low vision,
nystagmus, or a cultural norm. Ask your instructor for the **eye-contact accommodation** before your exam. With it,
the screen doesn't fade and the examiner doesn't ask you to look at it; it still notices if your face is out of
view. The indicator then says that eye contact isn't required for you. See [Accommodations](accommodations.md).

## If the check can't start

On some devices the eye-contact check can't run (for example an older device without the features it needs). The
indicator then says "Eye contact isn't being checked… The exam carries on." You don't need to do anything.

## Tips

- Put the light in front of you, not behind you.
- Keep the examiner's window where you naturally look; don't put it on a second screen off to the side.
- If you wear glasses that reflect a lot of light, tilt the screen slightly.
- If the screen fades while you were thinking, just look back; nothing is lost.
