# How a Gadfly exam works

Gadfly checks understanding the way a good oral examiner does: by talking with you. Here is the whole journey in
one page, whether you are a student about to sit an exam or someone deciding whether to use Gadfly.

## The short version

1. Your instructor uploads the course material and builds an exam on its topics.
2. You sign in, open **My exams** and press **Start**.
3. A get-ready page checks your browser, connection, **microphone and camera**, lets you try a practice question,
   and shows a plain-language notice to agree to.
4. A **talking examiner** asks you one question at a time, out loud. You answer out loud, on camera.
5. Each next question grows out of your last answer. The exam ends when the topics are covered, time runs out, or
   you choose to finish.
6. **People grade your answers.** An AI panel gives a preliminary view; an examiner scores independently; large
   differences are reconciled; an authorized person approves; then the result is published to you.

## Why spoken, and why on camera?

Explaining something out loud, and then answering a follow-up about what you just said, shows understanding in a
way a written answer can't. It is also much harder to fake: you can't paste an answer, and a memorized paragraph
falls apart at the first "why?".

That is why **every exam is spoken and the camera must be on**. There is no typing mode for students. (Typed
answers exist only as an accommodation an instructor grants; see [Accommodations](accommodations.md).)

The camera is for you to keep eye contact with the examiner, like in a real conversation. **No video is recorded
or sent**: the analysis happens on your own device, and only short notes (for example "looked away for 12 seconds")
are kept, for a person to read alongside your answer.

## Who decides your grade?

People do. The AI panel's scores are **preliminary**:

- an examiner scores each learning objective without seeing the AI's view first (blind grading is the default);
- where the AI and the examiner differ a lot, a reviewer reconciles the two;
- an authorized person (an instructor, admin or owner, and never the person who scored it) approves the grade;
- only then is it published to you, and you can appeal it.

Integrity notes (eye contact, tab switches, an answer that reads like copied text) are **never part of the grade**
and never fail anyone automatically. See [How grading and results work](grading-and-results.md).

## What do I need?

- A computer, or a tablet, with a **microphone and camera** (a headset with a microphone works best).
- A current **Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari**.
- A steady internet connection.
- A quiet place where you can talk for the length of the exam.

The [before-your-exam checklist](before-your-exam.md) walks you through it.
