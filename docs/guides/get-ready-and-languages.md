# Getting ready: the get-ready page and your languages

> 한국어로 읽기: [시험 준비와 언어](get-ready-and-languages-ko.md)
> Leer en español: [La página de preparación y sus idiomas](get-ready-and-languages-es.md)

When you press **Start** on an exam in **My exams**, you land on the get-ready page. Everything for your exam is on this one
screen: your languages, your camera and microphone, the examiner, how the exam works and the notice. **Nothing on this page is
graded or recorded, and no attempt is used until you press Begin exam**, so take your time. If you leave, you can come back
later.

## Your languages

Gadfly exams can be taken in **English, Korean or Spanish**, whichever your instructor offers for that exam.

The page asks three things:

1. **Which languages do you speak?** Tick every language you speak. Each language is shown in its own script.
2. **Which language do you speak best?** This is your *primary language*.
3. **Which language do you want to take this exam in?** You only see the languages this exam is offered in. If the exam has
   only one language, the page just says which.

Why we ask, and what happens with your answers:

- **It is never used to grade you.** The people and the AI panel who grade your answers are not told your primary language.
- **Nothing is worked out about you.** Gadfly never guesses your language, accent or where you come from from your voice, your
  name or your face. Only what you tell us here is used.
- **It makes the exam fairer for second-language speakers.** If you take an exam in a language that isn't the one you speak
  best (for example, Korean is your best language and the exam is in English), the exam adapts: the examiner speaks a little more
  slowly and plainly, the speech recognizer is held to the exam's language, and it waits longer before it decides you have
  finished an answer, because people pause more when they speak a second language. If the recognizer was unsure of a word in
  an answer that wasn't yet convincing, the examiner may ask you one short question about that word instead of guessing.
  If English is your best language and the exam is in English, nothing changes.
- Your answers are saved when you press **Begin exam** and filled in for you next time. You can change them here before any
  exam.

**Choosing the exam language.** When you pick a language, the page, the rules, the notice and the examiner's preview switch to
it straight away. You can change your mind until you press **Begin exam**; then the language is fixed for this attempt. (If you
change it after ticking the notice, the notice is rebuilt in the new language and you tick it again, so you always agree to the
words you read.)

**An exam in Korean.** The examiner asks the questions in Korean, listens in Korean and the lips move with the Korean sounds.
You answer in Korean; English technical terms in a Korean answer are fine. Your course material and the grading rubric stay as
your instructor wrote them, and your answers are judged against the same standard as an English attempt. If your instructor
hasn't checked the Korean title and instructions yet, the page says they were machine-translated. The exam room is in Korean
too: its buttons, status lines and notices, the examiner and the questions.

**An exam in Spanish** works the same way, in Spanish. The examiner asks in neutral Latin American Spanish and addresses you
as *usted*; "No lo sé" is an honest answer. The exam room and the student pages are in Spanish too. As with Korean, every
score on an exam taken in Spanish is approved by a person before it is released to you.

You can also switch the language of the student pages at any time with the language switch in the top bar (English / 한국어 /
Español).

## Camera and microphone

Allow your camera and microphone when your browser asks. Then:

- **Pick the camera and microphone you want to use** from the lists. The picture and the level meter switch to the one you
  choose, and your choice is remembered on this device for the exam.
- **Say a few words** so the meter moves. The microphone test also tells you if your room is noisy, your microphone is too
  loud (clipping), or the examiner's voice might echo back into it (use a headset if it says so).
- Wait until every check shows ✓. A **!** is a warning you can carry on with; a ✗ must be fixed, and the fix is written under
  it. Then press **Check again**. If something won't pass, see [microphone problems](troubleshooting-microphone.md),
  [camera problems](troubleshooting-camera.md) or [browser problems](troubleshooting-browser.md).

**What the camera is for.** During the exam the camera checks only that you are there, on your own, and looking at the screen.
It works on your own device: no video or picture of you is recorded or sent. The camera never grades you and never decides
anything. See [Eye contact and gentle reminders](eye-contact-and-the-screen-fade.md) and [Leaving the exam window](leaving-the-exam-window.md).

## Help the examiner hear you (voice calibration)

Under **Help the examiner hear you** you can read one short sentence aloud three times, in each language you will use:

- English: *The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.*
- Korean: *다람쥐 헌 쳇바퀴에 타고파.*
- Spanish: *El veloz murciélago hindú comía feliz cardillo y kiwi.*

It takes about a minute and helps the speech recognizer get used to your pace, so it waits for you and mishears you less. Only a
few numbers are kept on your language profile (how fast you read, how long your pauses were, how sure the recognizer was):
**never a recording, and never your words**. You can stop, skip or redo it. If English is your best language and the exam is in
English, it is folded away as optional.

## Meet your examiner

Your examiner is an AI. The examiner speaks each question, listens to your answer, and asks a follow-up built from your own
words.

- **Choose your examiner.** There are two examiners, a female one and a male one. They ask questions in exactly the same way,
  with the same rules; only the look and the voice differ, and both speak English, Korean and Spanish. Pick the one you prefer; your
  choice is remembered on this device for the exam room. If your instructor or organization has set the examiner for this
  exam, the page shows only that one.
- **Hear a hello.** The preview uses your browser's own voice, with the words lighting up as they are said. In the exam the
  examiner speaks with their own voice.
- **Make the examiner your own (optional).** Under the preview you can change the examiner's **voice**, **skin tone**, **hair
  colour**, **clothing** and **framing** (head and shoulders, close, or seated), so you sit the exam with someone you are
  comfortable talking to. Pick from the choices shown; **Reset to default** puts everything back. When you pick a voice the
  hello plays again, still in your browser's voice: the voice you chose is heard in the exam room and the practice run. Your
  choices are remembered on this device with your examiner and **never affect your grade**; graders don't see them.
  - **What stays the same, and why.** Only the skin, hair and clothing colours change. The examiner's eyes, eyebrows, lips and
    mouth are never changed, and every framing keeps the face and eyes in full view, so you can always look the examiner in the
    eye and the eye-contact check works exactly as usual. There are no masks, hats, glasses or anything that covers the face,
    and every voice is one Gadfly has checked in English, Korean and Spanish.
  - If your instructor or organization has set the examiner for this exam, the examiner appears exactly as they set it and
    this panel is not shown. If the exam has its own voice, that voice is used instead of the one you chose.
- **Try a free practice run.** The link opens the [practice interview](practice-interview.md) with this exam's own examiner
  voice. It is ungraded, unlimited and nothing from it is kept. A full rehearsal is the best way to calm nerves.

## How it works, and the notice

**How Socratic questioning works** explains the method in a few lines, with one practice question you can answer out loud
(not recorded). **Why it works this way** has the research behind it.

Then read the **notice**: what is recorded, what isn't, how long it is kept, who decides your result, and the rules for pauses
and time. Tick **I have read this notice and agree to take the exam on these terms** and press **Begin exam**. A fingerprint of
the exact words you agreed to, in the language you read them in, is kept with your exam.
