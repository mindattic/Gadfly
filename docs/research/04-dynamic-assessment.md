# Research note 04: Dynamic assessment and the zone of proximal development

Prepared 2026-11-21 for Gadfly (GAD-T254). Gadfly deliberately never hints while it scores: an unclear answer gets a neutral clarifying question, and each follow-up is written from the student's own previous answer, not from the model answer. Dynamic assessment (DA) is the family of methods that does the opposite on purpose: it builds help into the test and treats the student's response to that help as evidence, usually of "learning potential". This note checks the core DA sources (Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, Feuerstein's LPAD, Lidz, Haywood and Lidz, Campione and Brown's graduated prompts, Grigorenko and Sternberg's reviews, Lantolf and Poehner's interventionist/interactionist split, Wood, Bruner and Ross on scaffolding) and the evidence on predictive validity and reliability (Caffrey, Fuchs and Fuchs 2008 and later work). The question is whether there is a principled case for an optional graduated-prompt mode in which the number and level of prompts a student needs is itself scored, and how such a mode would have to be kept apart from the unhinted scored exam.

How to read the verification levels:
- **Full text read** - the cited passage was checked in the work itself (the part read is named).
- **Abstract/publisher page read** - claims are limited to what the abstract or landing page says.
- **Record located only** - the bibliographic record was confirmed, but the content was not read; anything said about content is marked as coming from a named secondary source.

## A. Foundations: the zone of proximal development and scaffolding

### Vygotsky1978
- **APA:** Vygotsky, L. S. (1978). *Mind in society: The development of higher psychological processes* (M. Cole, V. John-Steiner, S. Scribner, & E. Souberman, Eds.). Harvard University Press.
- **Link:** https://archive.org/download/levs.vygotskymindinsocietythedevelopmentzlib.org/ (OCR text file of the Harvard University Press edition, "Copyright 1978 ... Second printing, 1979"); https://www.marxists.org/archive/vygotsky/works/1931/school-age.htm (2017 Mitchell translation of the source essay, for comparison)
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Full text read - chapter 6, "Interaction between Learning and Development", pp. 85-87 (title page and copyright page also checked). The copy is a community upload on archive.org, not a publisher copy; the OCR text was read, not page images. The Mitchell translation page was opened for comparison only.
- **Verified quote or claim:** "This difference between twelve and eight, or between nine and eight, is what we call the zone of proximal development. It is the distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers." (p. 86). On how the help is given: "Different experimenters might employ different modes of demonstration in different cases: some might run through an entire demonstration and ask the children to repeat it, others might initiate the solution and ask the child to finish it, or offer leading questions." (p. 86). And: "The actual developmental level characterizes mental development retrospectively, while the zone of proximal development characterizes mental development prospectively." (pp. 86-87).
- **What it establishes:** The ZPD is defined by contrasting unaided performance with performance under guidance, and Vygotsky's own illustration is two children with the same unaided level (8) who reach different levels with help (12 and 9). He argues that unaided testing describes development retrospectively and assisted performance describes it prospectively. The passage is explicit that the form of help was not standardized ("different experimenters might employ different modes").
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** This is the theoretical case that unaided scoring (what Gadfly does) measures only the "actual developmental level". It supports offering a separate assisted mode as additional information, not as a replacement. It does not by itself support a scored number of prompts: Vygotsky describes the gap, not a metric, and his help was deliberately varied.
- **Confidence:** High - exact wording and page checked in the 1978 edition text; Medium only on OCR fidelity of single characters.

## B. The main dynamic assessment traditions

### Feuerstein1979
- **APA:** Feuerstein, R., Rand, Y., & Hoffman, M. B. (1979). *The dynamic assessment of retarded performers: The Learning Potential Assessment Device, theory, instruments, and techniques*. University Park Press.
- **Link:** https://openlibrary.org/search.json?q=dynamic+assessment+of+retarded+performers (Open Library record: Feuerstein, 1979, University Park Press, ISBN 0839115059); https://feuerstein-institute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Ch.-3.-LPAD.pdf (Feuerstein, R., Falik, L. H., & Feuerstein, Ra., "The Learning Potential Assessment Device", undated chapter; footnote says an earlier version appeared in Samuda (Ed.), 1998, *Advances in Cross-Cultural Assessment*, Sage)
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** 1979 book: Record located only (subtitle wording varies between catalogues; not read). Feuerstein, Falik and Feuerstein chapter: Full text read (opening section; "The Structure of the Instruments"; the passage on mediation versus other interventions; the passage on graduated prompting before "Criteria to Evaluate Change").
- **Verified quote or claim:** From the chapter: "In 1979, the senior author, in a book entitled The Dynamic Assessment of Retarded Performers, presented the rationale for needed alternatives to conventional psychometric assessment, as well as a new approach to the assessment of learning potential, the Learning Potential Assessment Device (LPAD)." On conventional tests, instrument construction choices "all of which lead to the search for reliability, and ultimately for predictability". On graduated prompts: "Interactions that are limited in their mediational flexibility, such as graduated prompting (Bransford et al., 1987; Campione & Brown, 1987) or the testing the limits approach of Carlson and Wiedl (1978, 1979) will not make possible the detection of the microchanges which can be produced in the individual on a variety of levels." The chapter also proposes renaming the device the Learning Propensity Assessment Device.
- **What it establishes:** The LPAD tradition treats assessment as mediated interaction aimed at producing and observing cognitive change, and deliberately trades the reliability-first design of conventional tests for sensitivity to change. Its own authors reject standardized graduated prompting as too inflexible for their purpose.
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** This is the strongest version of the "interactionist" case and the clearest challenge to any scripted prompt ladder. It also shows that the DA field itself does not agree that prompt counts are a good measure. For Gadfly, the LPAD model fits a tutoring/practice mode, not a scored, comparable, human-approved exam.
- **Confidence:** High for what the chapter says (read); Medium for attributing those positions to the 1979 book itself (book not read).

## C. Interventionist versus interactionist DA, and what a standardized prompt ladder looks like

### LantolfPoehner2004
- **APA:** Lantolf, J. P., & Poehner, M. E. (2004). Dynamic assessment of L2 development: Bringing the past into the future. *Journal of Applied Linguistics, 1*(1), 49-72. https://doi.org/10.1558/japl.1.1.49.55872
- **Link:** https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/dynamic-assessment-of-l2-development-bringing-the-past-into-the-f/ ; https://calper.la.psu.edu/?p=909 (2003 CALPER working paper version, landing page only)
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Abstract/publisher page read (Penn State research portal record with abstract; DOI confirmed against Crossref).
- **Verified quote or claim:** Paraphrase of the abstract: DA is grounded in the ZPD; its defining feature is that the examiner mediates performance through prompts, hints and leading questions, both to reveal abilities and to promote development during the assessment; the paper reviews DA approaches, notes how few studies addressed language learning, and discusses criticisms. The abstract page does not itself use the words "interventionist" or "interactionist".
- **What it establishes:** That this is the paper later L2 work cites for the interventionist/interactionist split (see Sarabi Asl et al. under Poehner2008), and that its framing makes mediation part of the assessment by design.
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** The framing directly conflicts with Gadfly's scored exam, where help is excluded on purpose. It supports a separate, labelled mediated mode, not mediation inside scoring.
- **Confidence:** Medium - abstract read; the split itself is attributed to this paper only through secondary citations.

### Poehner2008

- **Link:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/978-0-387-75775-9 (record; Springer page redirected to a login); https://sfleducation.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s40862-023-00237-x.pdf (Sarabi Asl, S., Rashtchi, M., & Rezaie, G. The effects of interactionist versus interventionist dynamic assessment models on Iranian EFL learners' speaking sub-skills: A mixed-method study. *Asian-Pacific Journal of Second and Foreign Language Education, 9*, Article 12. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40862-023-00237-x)
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Poehner 2008: Record located only (Crossref: title "Dynamic Assessment", Springer US, Educational Linguistics series, 2008; the subtitle above is as commonly cited and was not shown in the Crossref record). Sarabi Asl et al. 2024: Full text read (introduction and "DA main models" section, pp. 2-5).
- **Verified quote or claim:** Sarabi Asl et al. quote Poehner (2008, pp. 44-45) as saying that in the interventionist model "mediators are not free to respond to learners' needs as these become apparent during the procedure but must instead follow a highly scripted approach to mediation in which all prompts, hints, and leading questions have been arranged hierarchically" - seen in Sarabi Asl et al., not checked in Poehner. Sarabi Asl et al. themselves write that the pre-planned interpretation, "while bringing about the comfort of better generalizability and large-scale administration, deprives learners and instructors of co-creating a ZPD" (p. 5). Paraphrase of Sarabi Asl et al.: interventionist DA uses pre-planned mediation, implicit-to-explicit, to produce quantifiable results for comparison; interactionist DA lets mediation emerge from dialogue and does not define a fixed endpoint.
- **What it establishes:** The two-model distinction as the L2 DA literature uses it, and the explicit trade-off: scripted hierarchies buy comparability and scale; open mediation buys responsiveness but not comparability.
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** If Gadfly ever scores help-needed, only the interventionist form is compatible with its multi-grader, blind-review, reconciliation pipeline, because every student must face the same prompt ladder for the count to mean the same thing. An LLM improvising hints would be interactionist by construction, and its prompt counts would not be comparable across students.
- **Confidence:** Medium - the distinction is read in a 2024 peer-reviewed article; the Poehner quote is secondary.

### Stad2019
- **APA:** Stad, F. E., Wiedl, K. H., Vogelaar, B., Bakker, M., & Resing, W. C. M. (2019). The role of cognitive flexibility in young children's potential for learning under dynamic testing conditions. *European Journal of Psychology of Education, 34*(1), 123-146. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10212-018-0379-8 (online 9 April 2018)
- **Link:** https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10212-018-0379-8.pdf
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Full text read (abstract, introduction, Method "graduated prompting" procedure and Scoring section).
- **Verified quote or claim:** If a child could not solve an item after a general instruction, "they received help according to a standardised graduated prompting protocol that consisted of four prompts"; prompts went from metacognitive to step-by-step and "were only provided after an incorrect answer". Scoring used pre- and post-test accuracy "In addition, the number of hints children were provided with, and the type of hints they utilised". The introduction lists measures of potential for learning as "the amount and the type of feedback received during training, performance after training, the change in performance from the pre-training to post-training stage, and the transfer of learned skills".
- **What it establishes:** A current, concrete example of an interventionist graduated-prompt protocol: fixed number of prompts, fixed order, prompt only after an error, number and type of prompts recorded alongside unaided pre/post scores. Study population was 153 children aged 6-7 on a series-completion task.
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** Gives a template for a "Practice with hints" mode: a fixed per-objective ladder (metacognitive, then conceptual, then worked step), prompt only after an inadequate answer, log prompt count and level, then an unaided retest. It is evidence from young children on inductive-reasoning puzzles, not adults on open conceptual questions, so it shows feasibility of the format, not validity for Gadfly's content.
- **Confidence:** High for what the protocol was; Low for transfer to Gadfly's population and task type.

### Caffrey2008
- **APA:** Caffrey, E., Fuchs, D., & Fuchs, L. S. (2008). The predictive validity of dynamic assessment: A review. *The Journal of Special Education, 41*(4), 254-270. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022466907310366
- **Link:** https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0022466907310366 (record with full abstract); the Vanderbilt repository page returned HTTP 421.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Abstract/publisher page read (Crossref abstract, verbatim).
- **Verified quote or claim:** Mixed-methods review of 24 studies; 15 analysed quantitatively with Pearson correlations. Analyses compared traditional tests and DA, compared two forms of DA, examined student populations and outcome types, and assessed DA's value added over traditional testing. "Results indicated superior predictive validity for DA when feedback is not contingent on student response, when applied to students with disabilities rather than at-risk or typically achieving students, and when independent DA and criterion-referenced tests were used as outcomes instead of norm-referenced tests and teacher judgment."
- **What it establishes:** DA's predictive advantage is conditional, not general. The abstract singles out non-contingent (standardized) feedback as the condition with better prediction, which favours scripted prompts over responsive ones for prediction purposes. - **Relevance to Gadfly:** Directly relevant to the design choice: if a hinted mode is ever used to predict anything, a fixed prompt ladder is better supported than LLM-improvised help. - **APA:** De Beer, M. (2006). Dynamic testing: Practical solutions to some concerns. *SA Journal of Industrial Psychology, 32*(4), 8-14. https://doi.org/10.4102/sajip.v32i4.245
- **Link:** https://sajip.co.za/index.php/sajip/article/download/245/242
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-21
- **Verification:** Full text read (abstract, discussion of Grigorenko and Sternberg's concerns, "Clarification of the concept of learning potential", IRT/CAT sections).
- **Verified quote or claim:** "There is a paucity of published empirical data on the reliability and validity of dynamic testing." On gain scores: "the difference score or ZPD has often (incorrectly) been referred to as that which indicates 'potential'"; the author argues the improvement score "should not be used alone, but that present level of performance as well as the projected future level of performance and the resulting difference score should also be taken into account." The abstract lists the remedies: standardised procedures, Item Response Theory for measurement accuracy, and computerised adaptive testing for time efficiency.
- **What it establishes:** A clear statement of the measurement problem: raw gain or help-needed scores are confounded with starting level (a high scorer has little room to gain), and DA needs standardization, a proper measurement model and time-efficient delivery to be usable.
- **Relevance to Gadfly:** Any help-needed metric must be reported alongside the unaided level, never alone, and should be modelled (at minimum, conditioned on the unaided score) rather than shown as a raw count. This is a direct design constraint for a hinted mode.
- **Confidence:** High for what the paper says; Medium as a field-wide verdict (single author, one instrument).

## Corrections and cautions

- **ZPD wording.** The 1978 text reads "more capable peers" (plural). Paraphrases such as "more knowledgeable other" do not appear in this passage; "scaffold" does not appear anywhere in the Mind in Society text checked (0 matches). Scaffolding comes from Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976), not from Vygotsky, though the two are often merged.
- **Mind in Society chapter 6 is an edited translation.** The 2017 Mitchell translation of the source essay (opened on marxists.org) gives a different example (two children of mental age seven, one reaching a nine-year-old's level with help, the other seven and a half) and speaks of the zone of "proximate" development. Quote the 1978 wording only as the 1978 edition's wording.
- **"DA predicts better than static tests" is overstated.** Caffrey et al. (2008) report superior prediction only under conditions (non-contingent feedback, students with disabilities, certain outcome types); their own group later summarised the review as finding that traditional tests and DA "similarly predicted" achievement. The defensible claim is that standardized DA can add some unique variance, not that it is generally better.
- **Responsive help is not the better-validated form.** It is often assumed that more responsive, tutor-like mediation is better assessment. For prediction, Caffrey et al.'s abstract points the other way (feedback not contingent on student response predicted better).
- **Gain or help-needed scores are not "potential".** De Beer (2006) calls this reading incorrect when initial levels differ; a raw count of prompts needed partly reflects where the student started.
- **The DA field does not endorse graduated prompts as a unit.** Feuerstein, Falik and Feuerstein explicitly say graduated prompting is too limited in mediational flexibility to detect the changes they care about.
- **Haywood and Lidz date.** Usually cited as 2007; Cambridge and Crossref give 18 December 2006 as the issued date. Either is defensible; give the DOI.
- **Campione and Brown page range.** Reference lists give pp. 82-115 and pp. 82-113; not resolved without the book.
- **Interventionist/interactionist attribution.** The 2004 Lantolf and Poehner abstract does not use the two labels; the attribution rests on later citations (e.g., Sarabi Asl et al., 2024) and on Poehner (2008), which was not read.
- **"Sandwich" and "cake" formats.** Commonly attributed to Sternberg and Grigorenko (2002); not found in any page opened for this note and not relied on.

## Synthesis for Gadfly

- **Keep the scored exam unhinted.** Nothing here undermines it. The ZPD argument (Vygotsky 1978) is that unaided performance is incomplete information, not that it is wrong; the predictive-validity evidence (Caffrey et al. 2008; Fuchs et al. 2011) shows DA is roughly comparable to static measures overall with some added value, not a replacement. The neutral clarifying question should stay strictly non-hinting so the unaided score keeps meaning what it says.
- **There is a principled case for an optional, separate "Practice with hints" mode.** Vygotsky's prospective-versus-retrospective argument, the test-intervene-retest model (Haywood & Lidz), and current graduated-prompt protocols (Stad et al. 2019) all support recording how a student responds to help as additional evidence about readiness to learn.
- **If help-needed is ever scored, it must be interventionist.** Comparability across students, blind human grading and reconciliation all require the same prompt ladder for everyone: a fixed, instructor-authored sequence per objective (for example metacognitive, then conceptual pointer, then worked step), given only after an inadequate answer. The LLM may choose when an answer is inadequate but must not write the hint text. Caffrey et al.'s finding that non-contingent feedback predicted better supports this.
- **Free-form LLM tutoring is interactionist and should not produce a score.** It can be valuable for learning (the Feuerstein/LPAD and interactionist L2 tradition), but its prompt counts are not comparable between students and should be shown, if at all, as a qualitative transcript note.
- **Never report a raw prompt count alone.** Report it alongside the unaided level and, preferably, an unaided retest on fresh items (transfer). De Beer (2006) shows gain and help-needed scores are confounded with starting level; the transfer criterion goes back to the early Campione/Brown and Lidz-volume work.
- **Treat any "learning potential" number as experimental.** The field's own reviews report thin and inconsistent reliability evidence (Grigorenko & Sternberg 1998, as reported by De Beer 2006). Before a help-needed metric reaches a released result, Gadfly would need its own data: inter-rater agreement on "inadequate answer" decisions, test-retest stability of prompt counts, and whether prompts-needed adds anything beyond the unaided score in predicting later performance.
- **Hard separation in data and UI.** Hinted sessions should be a different session type, never merged into exam scores, never visible to blind graders of the scored exam, and labelled as practice on any report. Integrity review should also know which mode a transcript came from, since hint-driven improvement in practice is expected, not suspicious.
- **Budget the cost.** Fuchs et al. report 20-30 minutes for one scripted DA task with young children; each prompt adds voice turns. A hinted mode is better as an opt-in practice or diagnostic step after the scored exam than as a default.
- **What is not supported:** that DA validity evidence from young children, students with disabilities and inductive-reasoning or decoding tasks carries over to adult Socratic exams on conceptual content. None of the sources tested that. Any Gadfly hinted mode would be a pilot needing its own validation.
