# Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, 1997

## Harry's start and his entry into the wizarding world

Harry Potter is an orphan raised by his Muggle aunt and uncle, **Vernon and Petunia Dursley**, and their son **Dudley**. They keep him in the cupboard under the stairs, treat him as a burden and hide the truth about his parents, because Petunia resents and fears the magic that her sister **Lily** had. Harry does not know that he is famous. As a baby he survived the **killing curse** from **Lord Voldemort**, who murdered his parents, **Lily and James Potter**, and then vanished when the curse rebounded. The scar on Harry's forehead is the mark of that night.

Hogwarts letters arrive in growing numbers until **Rubeus Hagrid**, the gamekeeper, breaks into the hut on the rock where the Dursleys have fled and tells Harry he is a wizard. Hagrid takes him to **Diagon Alley** to buy a wand, books and an owl, and Harry learns he has gold at **Gringotts**, the goblin bank, left by his parents. The wand that chooses him, holly with a phoenix feather, shares its core with Voldemort's, and **Ollivander** calls this curious. Harry boards the Hogwarts Express at **Platform Nine and Three-Quarters** and meets **Ron Weasley**, who is poor and one of many brothers, and later **Hermione Granger**, a brilliant Muggle-born who at first comes across as a know-it-all.

## Hogwarts, houses and friendship

At Hogwarts the **Sorting Hat** weighs Harry's choice and places him in **Gryffindor**. **Draco Malfoy**, who has already tried to befriend him, becomes his rival after Harry refuses his offer of friendship. The headmaster, **Albus Dumbledore**, is the kind but mysterious guardian of the school, and **Professor McGonagall** is the strict head of Gryffindor. Harry also finds he is a natural **Seeker** on the Quidditch team, and he receives a **Nimbus 2000** broom.

The friendship of the three children forms after they face a **mountain troll** in the girls' bathroom on Halloween. Hermione lies to the teachers to protect the boys, and this shared danger and loyalty bind them together. The book presents Harry as ordinary in character, a child who wants a family and a place to belong. His kindness to Ron, his unwillingness to be a bully and his preference for friends over fame help set him apart from Draco. Hermione's knowledge and Ron's skill at chess prove as important as Harry's courage.

## The mystery of the stone

A package from Gringotts, emptied from **vault 713** on the day Harry was there, leads the three friends to a series of clues. They find that **Fluffy**, a three-headed dog, guards a trapdoor on the forbidden third floor. Hagrid lets slip that the object belongs to **Nicolas Flamel**, a famed alchemist, and Hermione discovers in a library book that Flamel made the **Philosopher's Stone**. The stone turns metal to gold and produces the **Elixir of Life**, which keeps its owner alive.

Harry suspects **Professor Snape**, the sour Potions master who clearly dislikes him. The book builds this as a red herring: Snape is cold and unfair, but he is not the villain. Several clues point at him, including a bitten leg that Harry sees and a Quidditch match in which Harry's broom bucks. In fact it is **Professor Quirrell**, the timid Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher, who curses the broom, while Snape mutters a counter-curse to protect Harry. Another ordeal comes from Hagrid, who wins an illegal dragon egg from a hooded stranger. The stranger is Quirrell, and he tricks Hagrid into revealing how to get past Fluffy, which is to play music.

## The trials and the confrontation

Fearing Snape will steal the stone, Harry, Ron and Hermione go through the trapdoor, past tests set by the teachers who guard it:

- **Devil's Snare**, a plant that tightens around those who struggle, is beaten by Hermione, who remembers it hates light.
- **Flying keys** guarding a door require Harry's skill as a Seeker.
- A life-sized **wizard's chess** game requires Ron to direct the pieces and sacrifice himself.
- A potions riddle of logic is solved by Hermione, who sends Harry on alone.

Harry reaches the last chamber and finds Quirrell, who admits he wants the stone, and that Voldemort, weak and without a body, lives on the back of his head under the turban. Quirrell cannot take the stone from the **Mirror of Erised**, which shows the viewer's deepest desire, because he sees only himself with it. Harry looks in and sees himself with the stone in his pocket. Dumbledore explains that the stone can only be got by someone who wants to find it but not to use it. Harry gets it because his desire is not for himself. Quirrell tries to kill him and cannot touch him, because **Lily's sacrificial love** left a protection that burns anyone driven by hatred. Quirrell dies as Voldemort leaves his body and flees.

## Key characters and their motives

- **Quirrell** is a once-ordinary teacher who went abroad, met Voldemort in Albania and was overpowered by his promises of power and by fear. He serves Voldemort partly from ambition and partly because he cannot refuse.
- **Voldemort** is driven by the wish to regain a body and to live forever, which is why he wants the stone. He sees every person as a tool, and he drinks **unicorn blood** in the Forbidden Forest to stay alive, an act Firenze the centaur calls a crime that brings a cursed half-life.
- **Snape** acts as protector though he despises Harry, and the book hints at an unexplained debt to James Potter.
- **Dumbledore** allows the children to reach the final chamber, trusting Harry to meet the test himself, and he left the stone as a trap that only the right person could take.
- **Hagrid** is loyal and warm, but his love of dangerous creatures and his talkativeness make him easy to trick.
- **Hermione** and **Ron** supply the logic and the strategy, and their roles show that no hero succeeds alone.

## Themes and magical concepts

- **Love as protection:** Dumbledore explains that Lily's act of dying to save her son marked Harry. The same mark is why Quirrell's skin blisters at his touch. Voldemort cannot understand love, and so he cannot see what has stopped him.
- **Choices over abilities:** Harry was nearly a Slytherin, but he chose otherwise. Neville is later rewarded for standing up to his friends, and Dumbledore says it takes courage to stand up to enemies and even more to stand up to friends.
- **Desire and temptation:** The **Mirror of Erised** warns against living in dreams. It shows Harry his family, and Dumbledore tells him that the mirror gives neither knowledge nor truth.
- **Death:** Dumbledore tells Harry that death is "the next great adventure" for a well-organised mind. The stone is destroyed, and Flamel and his wife choose to die, a view that stands against Voldemort's fear of dying.
- **Appearances deceive:** the apparently sinister Snape protects Harry, and the stuttering Quirrell is the traitor.

## The ending and what it sets up

Dumbledore awards last-minute points to Ron, Hermione, Harry and **Neville**, and Gryffindor beats Slytherin for the **House Cup**, ending Slytherin's long run of wins. Harry goes back to the Dursleys for the summer, now knowing he belongs elsewhere. Voldemort has not been destroyed: he escaped as a spirit and will look for another way to return. The book sets up Harry's rivalry with Draco, the mystery of Snape's hatred and the lasting threat of Voldemort.
