# Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban, 1999

## The escaped prisoner

Harry's third summer ends when he loses his temper at his uncle's sister **Marge** and inflates her with an accidental spell. He runs away and is picked up by the **Knight Bus**. **Cornelius Fudge**, the Minister for Magic, does not punish him, because a bigger concern has arisen: **Sirius Black** has escaped from **Azkaban**, the wizard prison guarded by **Dementors**. Black is believed to have betrayed Lily and James Potter to Voldemort and to have murdered **Peter Pettigrew** and twelve Muggles with a single curse. He is the first person to escape Azkaban, and the common belief is that he is hunting Harry.

The Ministry posts Dementors around Hogwarts to guard the school. They drain happiness from everyone near them, and Harry reacts worse than others, hearing the last moments of his mother's life. The book treats this as a depiction of depression and fear: the Dementors do not attack with force but feed on despair, and their worst weapon is the **Kiss**, which removes a person's soul.

## Lupin, boggarts and the Patronus

The new Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher is **Remus Lupin**, who is shabby, gentle and kind to Harry. His first lesson uses a **boggart**, a shapeshifter that takes the form of what the viewer fears most, and he teaches that laughter defeats it with the **Riddikulus** charm. When it faces Harry the boggart turns into a Dementor, showing that what Harry truly fears is fear itself. Lupin privately teaches him the **Patronus Charm**, which conjures a guardian from a happy memory, the only defence against Dementors. Harry's Patronus is a **stag**.

Other teachers fill out the year. **Professor Trelawney**, the Divination teacher, predicts deaths in vague terms and sees a **Grim**, an omen of death, in Harry's tea leaves. Hagrid becomes teacher of Care of Magical Creatures and introduces the **hippogriff Buckbeak**. When Draco provokes Buckbeak and gets scratched, the Malfoys press for the animal's execution, and Hagrid, unsure of himself, bungles the defence.

Hermione, meanwhile, carries a heavy timetable because she has been given a **Time-Turner**, a device for going back in time.

## What really happened

Harry gets the **Marauder's Map** from Fred and George. It shows everyone in the castle, and it was made by four friends nicknamed **Moony, Wormtail, Padfoot and Prongs**. Sirius has already broken into Gryffindor tower once and slashed the portrait of the Fat Lady, and Ron's pet rat **Scabbers** has gone missing, supposedly eaten by Hermione's cat **Crookshanks**. Near the end of the year a large black dog seizes Ron and drags him through the Whomping Willow's tunnel to the **Shrieking Shack**. Harry and Hermione follow, and there the dog becomes Sirius, who is **Harry's godfather**. Lupin, who had seen Pettigrew's name on the map, arrives soon after.

The truth emerges. Sirius, Lupin, Pettigrew and **James** were close friends at school. Lupin is a **werewolf**, and the others became unregistered **Animagi** (Sirius a dog, Pettigrew a rat, James a stag) so they could keep him company at the full moon. The Potters had used the **Fidelius Charm** and made Sirius their **Secret Keeper**, then secretly switched to Pettigrew, believing no one would suspect the weaker friend. Pettigrew sold them to Voldemort.

When Sirius found him, Pettigrew blew up the street, cut off his own finger, transformed and escaped, and Sirius was blamed and jailed without trial. Scabbers is Pettigrew, who has lived as Ron's rat for twelve years, hiding from both sides.

Snape, who has hated Sirius and Lupin since school, bursts in under the Invisibility Cloak, and the three students knock him out, because he is sure Sirius is guilty and will not listen.

## Key characters and their motives

- **Sirius Black** was framed and spent twelve years in Azkaban. He escapes because he learned Pettigrew was alive and near Harry, and his motive is to protect his godson and clear his name. He survived the Dementors by remaining aware he was innocent.
- **Remus Lupin** is a good man hampered by his condition and by the fear of exposure. He wavers between loyalty to his friends and his duty to Dumbledore, and he fails to take his **Wolfsbane Potion** at a vital moment.
- **Peter Pettigrew** is a coward who joined the stronger side, first Voldemort and later hiding from him, a pattern of choosing safety over loyalty.
- **Severus Snape** hates Sirius and Lupin from schooldays, and his zeal to catch them shows a grudge that clouds his judgment.
- **Cornelius Fudge** wants to look in control, and so uses the Dementors and condemns people without trial.
- **Hermione** carries the strain of overwork, and her loyalty to Buckbeak and her friends tests her rule-following.

## Mercy, time and the rescue

- **Mercy:** Lupin and Sirius mean to kill Pettigrew, but Harry stops them. He argues his father would not have wanted them to become killers, and that Pettigrew should face justice. Dumbledore later tells him that Pettigrew now owes him a **life debt**, which will matter.
- **Judgment and prejudice:** Everyone judges on partial information, whether about Sirius, Snape, Lupin or Buckbeak. Lupin resigns when his lycanthropy is revealed to the school, a mark of how wizards shun werewolves.
- **Time:** Dumbledore tells Hermione and Harry to use the Time-Turner to save "more than one innocent life". Pettigrew transforms and escapes while Lupin transforms at the full moon, and Sirius is captured and sentenced to receive the Kiss.

Hermione and Harry go back three hours. They free Buckbeak before his execution and use him to reach Sirius's cell.

On the lakeshore, Harry watches himself being attacked by Dementors and sees a **stag Patronus** drive them off. He believes it is his father. In his earlier time he realises that it was himself, and in the second pass he casts the spell. Sirius escapes on Buckbeak.

The book teaches that the person we hope will rescue us may be the one we become.

## The ending and what it sets up

Snape, who had taken the credit he wanted for catching Sirius, is furious that Sirius escapes, and Lupin resigns and leaves Hogwarts. Trelawney makes a real prophecy in a trance that the Dark Lord's servant will return to him, which comes true when Pettigrew flees to Voldemort. Sirius, free but still hunted, gives Harry a chance of a family: he writes inviting Harry to live with him in future. Harry goes back to the Dursleys, and a Firebolt broom from Sirius is the book's last gift. The book closes with Voldemort's loyal servant at large, which sets up the return of the Dark Lord.
