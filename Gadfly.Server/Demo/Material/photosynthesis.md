# Photosynthesis

## Overview
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells. The overall equation is 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2. The oxygen released comes from splitting water, not from carbon dioxide.

## Light-dependent reactions
The light-dependent reactions happen in the thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll absorbs light, mostly red and blue wavelengths, and reflects green, which is why leaves look green. The absorbed energy excites electrons, which pass along an electron transport chain. Water is split (photolysis) to replace those electrons, releasing oxygen as a by-product. The energy is captured as ATP and NADPH.

## The Calvin cycle
The Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions) happens in the stroma. It uses the ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. The enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO2 to a five-carbon molecule, RuBP. The cycle does not need light directly, but it stops soon after dark because it depends on the ATP and NADPH the light reactions make.

## Limiting factors
The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, or temperature. Raising a factor that is not limiting does not increase the rate. Temperature matters because the Calvin cycle is enzyme-driven; too hot and enzymes such as RuBisCO denature.
