# The Harry Potter Series, 1997–2007

## The hidden world and its divisions

J. K. Rowling's seven books are set in a **wizarding world** that exists secretly inside ordinary Britain. A law of secrecy keeps it hidden from **Muggles**, the non-magical majority, and the **Ministry of Magic** enforces that law along with the rest of magical government. The Ministry is often cautious, image-conscious and slow to admit danger, which matters a great deal to the plot.

Young witches and wizards learn magic at **Hogwarts**, a boarding school run by the headmaster **Albus Dumbledore**. Students are sorted into four houses, each prizing a trait: **Gryffindor** (courage), **Slytherin** (ambition and cunning), **Ravenclaw** (learning) and **Hufflepuff** (loyalty and fair play). The houses compete for a cup, and the rivalry between Gryffindor and Slytherin runs through the series. The Sorting Hat takes a student's choices into account, and Harry, who could have gone to Slytherin, is placed in Gryffindor partly because he asks not to be sorted elsewhere.

The central social problem is **blood-status prejudice**. Some old families, such as the Malfoys, prize "pure-blood" ancestry and look down on **Muggle-borns**, whom they insult with a slur, and on **half-bloods**. Rowling uses this to show how prejudice works: it is a story people tell to protect their own power, and it is false, since magical ability appears in Muggle-born children as readily as anywhere else. The same pattern appears in how wizards treat **house-elves**, goblins and werewolves.

## Voldemort and the Horcruxes

The villain, **Lord Voldemort**, was born **Tom Riddle**, the son of a Muggle father and a witch from the Gaunt family, who descended from Salazar Slytherin. He grew up in an orphanage, cold and unloved, and came to Hogwarts as a brilliant, charming student who hid his cruelty. He reinvented himself, despised his Muggle blood, and adopted the name Voldemort because he wanted to be feared and to escape his ordinary origins.

His defining motive is **fear of death**. Where Harry eventually learns to accept death, Voldemort treats it as the ultimate insult and spends his life trying to avoid it. His method is the **Horcrux**, an object in which a wizard hides a fragment of his soul, made by splitting the soul through an act of murder. As long as a Horcrux survives, he cannot truly die. This is why the books treat his project as monstrous: each Horcrux costs him a part of his humanity, and he ends up less than human. His Horcruxes include Riddle's diary, the Gaunt family ring, Slytherin's locket, Hufflepuff's cup, Ravenclaw's diadem, his snake **Nagini**, and, unknown even to him, a piece lodged inside Harry.

His followers are the **Death Eaters**, who share his blood-purity ideology or simply want power and safety. Their loyalty rests on fear, not affection, and Voldemort treats them as tools.

## Harry's story, book by book

- **Philosopher's Stone (1997):** Harry, raised unhappily by his Muggle relatives the Dursleys, learns he is a wizard and the "boy who lived", who survived Voldemort's attack as a baby. With **Ron Weasley** and **Hermione Granger** he stops Quirrell, who carries the weakened Voldemort on the back of his head, from taking the stone.
- **Chamber of Secrets (1998):** The Chamber is opened and Muggle-borns are attacked. Harry defeats a basilisk and destroys Riddle's diary, an early Horcrux, and learns he can speak to snakes, a trait that makes people suspect him.
- **Prisoner of Azkaban (1999):** Everyone believes **Sirius Black** escaped prison to kill Harry. In truth Sirius is Harry's godfather, framed for betraying the Potters, and the real traitor is **Peter Pettigrew**, who hid as Ron's pet rat. The book is about mistaken judgments and the way mercy toward Pettigrew has consequences later.
- **Goblet of Fire (2000):** Harry is entered into the Triwizard Tournament, and the contest is a trap that returns Voldemort to a body. Cedric Diggory is killed. The Ministry refuses to believe Harry, and the series turns darker.
- **Order of the Phoenix (2003):** The Ministry denies Voldemort's return and attacks Harry and Dumbledore, installing the controlling Dolores Umbridge at Hogwarts. Harry forms Dumbledore's Army. A trap at the Ministry costs Sirius his life, and Harry learns the **prophecy** about himself and Voldemort.
- **Half-Blood Prince (2005):** Dumbledore shows Harry memories of Riddle's past to uncover the Horcruxes. Draco Malfoy, forced by Voldemort, lets Death Eaters into the school, and Snape kills Dumbledore.
- **Deathly Hallows (2007):** Harry, Ron and Hermione leave school to hunt the Horcruxes, and the story ends in the Battle of Hogwarts.

## Characters and their motives

**Dumbledore** is wise and kind but flawed. In his youth he and **Gellert Grindelwald** dreamed of wizards ruling over Muggles "for the greater good", and Dumbledore's sister Ariana died in a quarrel connected to that friendship. This history explains his distrust of his own hunger for power. He refuses the post of Minister, and he keeps secrets from Harry, partly out of love and partly out of habit, which sometimes costs Harry dearly.

**Severus Snape** seems to be a bully and a Death Eater. In fact he loved **Lily Potter** since childhood, and after Voldemort marked her for death he turned to Dumbledore and spent years protecting her son while resenting him as James's child. His patronus, a doe like Lily's, and his single word "Always" show that his loyalty was to Lily, not to a cause. He is not a simple hero: he is cruel to students. The series asks whether lasting love can excuse that.

**Hermione** is the brightest student and a Muggle-born who campaigns for house-elf rights. **Ron** is loyal and often insecure in the shadow of his brothers and his famous friend. Their steadiness is what lets Harry succeed, and the three show that friendship, not lone heroism, defeats Voldemort. The **Weasleys** are poor, pure-blood and welcoming, a counter-example to the Malfoys: family is defined by love and not by blood status.

**Neville Longbottom** is the timid boy who becomes brave. His parents were driven insane by Death Eaters, and the prophecy could equally have meant him. He later destroys Nagini with Gryffindor's sword. **Draco Malfoy** is Harry's rival, shaped by his father's beliefs and frightened when the task of killing Dumbledore falls to him. He hesitates, which shows his conscience. **Sirius** is Harry's nearest thing to a father, reckless and confined by years of unjust imprisonment. **Remus Lupin**, a werewolf shunned by society, is a gentle teacher who shows the cost of prejudice. **Dobby**, a house-elf freed by Harry, chooses freedom and loyalty and dies saving Harry's friends.

## Themes

- **Love and sacrifice:** Lily died to save Harry, and that act left a protection in his blood that Voldemort, who does not understand love, ignores. Voldemort consistently underrates it.
- **Choice and destiny:** The prophecy only matters because Voldemort acts on it. He picks Harry as his rival, though Neville might have fit. Dumbledore tells Harry that our choices show who we are far more than our abilities.
- **Death:** The **Deathly Hallows** are the Elder Wand, the Resurrection Stone and the Cloak of Invisibility, from a tale of three brothers who meet Death. Voldemort chases the wand for power over death. Harry values the Cloak, which hides without escaping, and accepts death.
- **Prejudice and power:** Muggle-born persecution, house-elf slavery and the Ministry's corruption under fear all show how the powerful use categories to excuse injustice.
- **Friendship and loyalty:** The heroes survive by trusting each other, while Voldemort's followers do not.

## The ending

At the **Battle of Hogwarts**, the defenders fight Voldemort's army. Harry learns from Snape's memories that he is himself a Horcrux, so he must die for Voldemort to be vulnerable. Harry walks into the forest and allows Voldemort to strike. Because Harry gives his life willingly for others, Voldemort cannot kill what he protects. Voldemort's spell destroys the Horcrux in Harry but leaves Harry able to return.

The **Elder Wand** passed to Draco, who disarmed Dumbledore, and then to Harry, who took the wand from Draco. Voldemort believes killing Snape would win him the wand. His mistake leaves the wand loyal to Harry, so Voldemort's own curse rebounds and kills him. The **epilogue**, nineteen years later, shows Harry, Ron and Hermione sending their children to Hogwarts, with Harry telling his son Albus Severus that he was named for two brave men, one of whom was a Slytherin.
