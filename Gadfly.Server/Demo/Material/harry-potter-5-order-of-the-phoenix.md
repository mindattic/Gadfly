# Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix, 2003

## Denial and the return of Voldemort

The fifth book opens a few weeks after the end of the previous one, when **Lord Voldemort** returned to a body and **Cedric Diggory** was killed. Harry, back with the Dursleys, is cut off from news and from his friends, and his isolation turns into anger. Two **Dementors** attack him and his cousin Dudley in Little Whinging, and Harry's use of a Patronus charm in front of a Muggle gives the Ministry the excuse to try to expel him.

The central political problem is **denial**. The Minister, **Cornelius Fudge**, will not accept that Voldemort is back, because accepting it would mean admitting his own failures and would threaten his hold on power. He fears most that **Dumbledore** wants his job. So the Ministry uses the newspaper the **Daily Prophet** to portray Harry as a liar and an attention-seeker and Dumbledore as a senile troublemaker. The book is about how a comfortable institution protects itself from an inconvenient truth by attacking those who tell it.

Dumbledore, meanwhile, revives the **Order of the Phoenix**, a secret group of adults who fought Voldemort in the first war. Its headquarters is **12 Grimmauld Place**, the gloomy ancestral house of **Sirius Black**, who must hide there because he is still officially a fugitive. Sirius hates being confined, and his restlessness is one of the book's quiet tragedies.

## Umbridge and Hogwarts under control

The Ministry places **Dolores Umbridge** at Hogwarts as Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher, then as **High Inquisitor** and finally as headmistress. She is polite, pink and sweetly spoken, and she is also one of the most hated characters in the series because her cruelty is bureaucratic and self-righteous. She issues a growing list of **Educational Decrees** that ban student societies, restrict teachers and control what is taught. She teaches only theory, so students will not be able to defend themselves, because the Ministry fears Dumbledore is raising an army. She punishes Harry with detention using a **blood quill**, which carves his own words ("I must not tell lies") into the back of his hand.

Her real motive is control, not education. Umbridge believes order and obedience matter more than truth, and she despises anyone she thinks is not fully human, including centaurs and half-breeds. Her fate fits her: after threatening the centaurs she is carried off by them in the Forbidden Forest.

Her opposite is **Dumbledore's Army**, a secret group that Harry, urged on by **Hermione**, teaches to practise real defensive spells in the **Room of Requirement**. Harry is a reluctant teacher at first, but he proves excellent because he has faced Voldemort. Members include **Neville**, **Luna Lovegood** and **Cho Chang**. It succeeds until **Marietta Edgecombe** betrays it, and a jinx Hermione placed on the sign-up list scars her face as a reminder. Dumbledore takes the blame to protect Harry and escapes. **Fred and George Weasley** leave school with a spectacular fireworks display that is the book's best act of rebellion.

## Occlumency and the link to Voldemort

Harry's scar hurts, and he sees through Voldemort's mind. In one vision he sees **Arthur Weasley** attacked by Voldemort's snake **Nagini**, which saves Arthur's life but also shows how deep the connection runs. Dumbledore, fearing that Voldemort could use the link to possess Harry, arranges for **Snape** to teach him **Occlumency**, the art of closing one's mind to outside intrusion, while Dumbledore himself avoids Harry's eyes.

The lessons fail. Snape is a hostile teacher, Harry fails to clear his mind of emotion, and he looks into Snape's most painful memory of being bullied by James and Sirius. The deeper reason is that Harry's strength comes from feeling, while Occlumency demands suppressing it. His emotions make him vulnerable to Voldemort's trap, a false vision of **Sirius being tortured**.

## The Department of Mysteries and the prophecy

Harry flies to the Ministry with Ron, Hermione, Ginny, Neville and Luna, ignoring Hermione's warning that it might be a trick. In the **Department of Mysteries** they find the **prophecy** orb that Voldemort wants, and a group of **Death Eaters** led by **Lucius Malfoy** ambushes them. Members of the Order arrive and a fierce battle follows. **Bellatrix Lestrange** kills Sirius, who falls through the **veil** in the death chamber. Harry tries to follow and is held back, then chases Bellatrix in rage. Voldemort appears in the Ministry atrium and duels Dumbledore before Fudge himself sees him, so denial is finally impossible.

Back at Hogwarts, Dumbledore finally tells Harry the truth. The prophecy, made by **Sybill Trelawney**, says that a boy born at the end of July to parents who had defied Voldemort three times would be marked as his equal, and that "neither can live while the other survives". Snape overheard the first part and passed it to Voldemort, who then chose Harry, a half-blood like himself, over Neville. Dumbledore admits he stayed away out of fear and affection, and that this was a mistake that cost Sirius.

## Themes and ending

- **Truth and institutional denial:** Fudge and Umbridge show how authority protects itself by silencing truth.
- **Anger, grief and isolation:** Harry's temper and his sense that nobody understands him are the emotional core. The book shows that his friends, not his solitude, are what help him.
- **Choice and prophecy:** The prophecy matters only because Voldemort believes it. Dumbledore stresses that Harry is not bound by it, and that he has something Voldemort lacks, the power of love.
- **Loss:** Sirius's death ends Harry's hope of a family and closes the book in grief. Harry ends the year grieving, but with loyal friends and a clear understanding of what he must do.

## Key terms to know

- **Order of the Phoenix:** Dumbledore's secret anti-Voldemort society, based at Grimmauld Place.
- **Educational Decrees:** Umbridge's rules that give the Ministry control of Hogwarts.
- **Blood quill:** the detention tool that scars students with their own writing.
- **Dumbledore's Army:** the student defence group that met in the Room of Requirement.
- **Occlumency and Legilimency:** closing one's mind versus reading another's.
- **Department of Mysteries:** the Ministry area holding the prophecy, the veil and the Hall of Prophecy.
- **The veil:** the archway through which Sirius falls, and from which no one returns.
- **The prophecy:** made by Sybill Trelawney, it ties Harry and Voldemort together.
- **Grawp and the centaurs:** Hagrid's giant half-brother and the herd that removes Umbridge.
- **Lucius Malfoy and Bellatrix:** the Death Eaters who lead the Ministry ambush.
