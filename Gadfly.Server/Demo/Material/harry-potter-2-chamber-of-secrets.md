# Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets, 1998

## The summer and the warning

Harry spends a miserable summer with the Dursleys until **Dobby**, a house-elf, appears in his room. Dobby has been intercepting Harry's mail and warns him not to return to Hogwarts, because terrible things will happen. He serves a wizarding family, though he does not say which, and his warning shows his divided loyalty: he is bound to his masters but wants to save Harry. When Harry refuses, Dobby wrecks the Dursleys' dinner party with a pudding, and Harry is locked in. Ron, **Fred** and **George** rescue him in a flying **Ford Anglia**, and he spends the rest of the summer at **the Burrow**, the Weasleys' crooked, warm home.

When the barrier at Platform Nine and Three-Quarters seals against them, Harry and Ron fly the car to Hogwarts and crash into the **Whomping Willow**. Snape threatens them with expulsion, though Dumbledore lets them stay.

The episode shows how rashly the boys act and how rules matter less to them than loyalty.

## The Chamber is opened

At Hogwarts the new Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher is **Gilderoy Lockhart**, vain, charming and famous for books about his adventures. He is a fraud: he took credit for the deeds of others and wiped their memories with a **Memory Charm**. His incompetence is comic but also exposes how readily a public trusts celebrity over substance.

Harry hears a cold voice in the walls, and soon **Mrs Norris**, the caretaker's cat, is found **Petrified** beneath a message in blood that reads "The Chamber of Secrets has been opened. Enemies of the heir, beware." Students learn the legend that **Salazar Slytherin**, one of the four founders of Hogwarts, built a hidden **Chamber** to hold a monster that his **heir** would one day unleash to purge the school of Muggle-borns, a slur he called "mudbloods". More victims follow, including **Colin Creevey**, **Justin Finch-Fletchley**, **Nearly Headless Nick** and finally **Hermione**.

## Suspicion, Parseltongue and the diary

Harry is the obvious suspect. At a dueling club he speaks **Parseltongue**, the language of snakes, and sets a snake on a boy. The school takes this as proof he is Slytherin's heir, and he is shunned. Harry himself fears it, since the Sorting Hat once considered placing him in Slytherin. The mistaken suspicion is a lesson in how a hidden trait leads people to a convenient but false conclusion, much like the prejudice that drives the attacks.

Harry and Ron find the haunted bathroom of **Moaning Myrtle**, a girl who died years ago, and a small black notebook. The notebook is the diary of **Tom Marvolo Riddle**, who attended Hogwarts fifty years earlier. When Harry writes in it, the diary writes back and shows him a memory: Riddle catching **Hagrid**, who was a student then, as the culprit behind the earlier attacks, when a girl died. Hagrid was expelled. Harry believes it, and when the Ministry takes Hagrid to Azkaban and the school governors suspend Dumbledore on the instigation of **Lucius Malfoy**, the situation looks dire. Hagrid's parting advice to follow the spiders leads Harry and Ron to **Aragog**, the giant spider Hagrid raised, who tells them Hagrid never opened the Chamber and the real monster is something else.

## The truth and the descent

Hermione, before she is petrified, works out that the monster is a **basilisk**, a giant serpent that kills with its glance. Nobody has died because everyone saw it indirectly: through water, a camera or a ghost. The spiders flee from it, and it travels through the **pipes**. She leaves the page of a library book clenched in her hand. **Ginny Weasley**, Ron's younger sister, is taken into the Chamber, and Harry and Ron go down through Myrtle's bathroom, with the fraudulent Lockhart, who tries to wipe their memories and has his own wand backfire.

Harry finds Ginny unconscious and meets **Riddle**, now a projection from the diary. Ginny had been writing to the diary all year, confiding her troubles, and Riddle, drawing on her life force, possessed her and made her open the Chamber. He reveals he is **Voldemort**, and that the name is an anagram of his birth name. He speaks Parseltongue and ordered the basilisk, while Harry's own ability is explained as a result of the night when Voldemort failed to kill him.

Riddle's motive is to finish what he started and kill the boy who defeated him. Harry is aided by **Fawkes**, Dumbledore's phoenix, who blinds the basilisk, and the **Sorting Hat**, which provides the **sword of Godric Gryffindor**. Harry kills the basilisk but is bitten by a fang.

Fawkes heals him with tears, and Harry stabs the diary with the fang. Riddle dies and Ginny wakes.

## Key characters and their motives

- **Tom Riddle** acts from pride and hatred of his Muggle ancestry. He is a preserved memory of his sixteen-year-old self, charming to Ginny until he no longer needs her.
- **Ginny Weasley** is shy and lonely in her first year, which makes her vulnerable. She is a victim, not a villain, and she is wracked by guilt afterwards.
- **Dobby** wants to protect Harry at the cost of punishing himself for betraying his masters, and the elf's cruel treatment shows how enslaved creatures are expected to accept abuse.
- **Lockhart** wants glory, not truth, and his flight when a real danger arises exposes him.
- **Hermione** shows resourcefulness, using the library and Polyjuice Potion to investigate, and she is petrified because she works out the truth first.
- **Lucius Malfoy** uses his wealth and position on the school governors to pursue his own interests.

## Themes, resolution and Dobby

- **Prejudice:** Riddle's attacks target Muggle-borns, and the diary shows blood-purity ideology producing violence.
- **Choice over origin:** Dumbledore tells Harry that Parseltongue and a likeness to Voldemort do not decide who he is, because "it is our choices that show what we truly are, far more than our abilities." The sword appears to those who are loyal to Dumbledore.
- **Dangers of trust:** Ginny confided in an object that could think for itself, and Mr Weasley's warning not to trust anything if you cannot see where it keeps its brain is the book's moral about whom, or what, to confide in.
- **Fame versus worth:** Lockhart's celebrity is empty, while Harry's reluctant courage is real.

**Lucius Malfoy** had slipped the diary into Ginny's cauldron to discredit Arthur Weasley and to seed trouble. Harry tricks him into freeing **Dobby** by hiding the diary in a sock that Lucius throws away, which frees the elf. Mandrake potions revive the petrified, Hagrid returns, and the exams are cancelled. The book closes with Hermione back and the school restored. It leaves the lasting problem that Riddle's diary was a surviving piece of Voldemort, and that Lucius is a Death Eater free to try again.
